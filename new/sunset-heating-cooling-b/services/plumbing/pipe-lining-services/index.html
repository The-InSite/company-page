Pipe Lining vs Pipe Replacement: Which is Better?

Pipe lining and pipe replacement both have their advantages, but the lining method is often preferred due to its trenchless nature, which means minimal excavation and disruption. Pipe lining is usually faster and more cost-effective, as it avoids the need for extensive digging and landscape restoration. Replacement, while sometimes necessary for old pipes, severely damaged sewer lines, or other damaged pipes, is more invasive and can be more expensive and time-consuming, but in the end, you’ll have completely new pipes. What Materials Are Used for Pipe Lining?

The primary material used in pipe lining is a flexible liner, usually made from fiberglass or polyester, which is coated with a two-part epoxy resin. This combination creates a durable, seamless new pipe inside the existing one once cured. The epoxy resin is known for its long-lasting properties and resistance to corrosion and root intrusion. Once installed and cured, the pipe lining becomes a permanent part of your plumbing system and is not intended to be removed. What Issues Can Occur When Pipe Lining Isn't Installed Properly?

If pipe lining is not installed correctly, several issues can arise. These include uneven curing, which can lead to weak spots or leaks, improper adhesion to the existing pipe, and misalignment, which can restrict water flow. Additionally, inadequate cleaning of the original pipe before lining can prevent the resin from bonding properly, leading to potential failures.
